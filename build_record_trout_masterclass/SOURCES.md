# Sources for the record trout masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the voiceover and caption, with where it comes from. Lines marked "angler
technique" are common angling advice, not research. The rainbow trout (modelled about 1.07 m with the tail, near the
record's reported 42 in, but illustrative), the lake, its rocky shoreline and slope (the cross section is not to
scale), the baitfish, the crankbait, the boat, rod and reel, and the strike are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) in November 2026; the notes
paraphrase those excerpts. Lines of the brief's script that the sources did not support were rewritten (see
"Changed from the brief" at the end).

## Beat 1: the record (hook)
- "In 2009, Sean Konrad landed a 48 pound rainbow trout from Saskatchewan's Lake Diefenbaker" / "IGFA ALL TACKLE
  RECORD · SEPT 5, 2009": Outdoor Life reports Sean Konrad's 48 lb rainbow trout, caught on 5 September 2009 on Lake
  Diefenbaker, Saskatchewan, 42 in long with a 32 in girth; Field and Stream reported it in 2009 as a pending all
  tackle world record.
  https://www.outdoorlife.com/fishing/biggest-trout-ever-caught/
  https://www.fieldandstream.com/photos/gallery/fishing/trout-fishing/where-fish-trout/2009/09/new-pending-all-tackle-world-record-ra
- "The IGFA lists it as the all tackle world record" / "IGFA · OUTDOOR LIFE · FIELD AND STREAM": Outdoor Life, Field
  and Stream and Flyfisher Pro report it as the IGFA all tackle rainbow trout record (48 lb, 21.77 kg).
  https://fieldandstream.com/stories/fishing/freshwater-fishing/trout-fishing/biggest-trout-ever-caught
  https://flyfisherpro.com/world-record/rainbow-trout/
  The IGFA records search itself was not opened, so the record's status in November 2026 was not confirmed against
  it. Search excerpts say the IGFA lists the method as casting, while Field and Stream describes back trolling (see
  Beat 3); the film shows trolling.
- Pin "rainbow trout": label of the simulated fish.

## Beat 2: where they hunt, badge [1] FIND THE BAITFISH
- "The lake's giant rainbows hunt schools of baitfish" / "big rainbows hunt the bait schools" / "HOOKED MAGAZINE ·
  IN FISHERMAN": Hooked Magazine describes the lake's giant trout pushing schools of cisco to the surface; In
  Fisherman's Saskatchewan article covers big trolled baits for the region's trophy trout.
  https://hookedmagazine.ca/huge-summertime-rainbows-on-cranks
  https://hookedmagazine.ca/late-fall-on-lake-diefenbaker
  https://in-fisherman.com/editorial/saskatchewan-fishing-land-of-giants/153464
- "Anglers find them along big, rocky shorelines" / "Big, rocky shorelines." / "HOOKED MAGAZINE": Hooked Magazine
  recommends covering water by trolling, starting with large sections of rocky shoreline where the lake's two arms
  meet (same Hooked pages as above; the excerpt did not say which of the two).
- ", where the bait gathers" (end of vo2): unsourced script wording. The user chose to keep vo2 as generated. No
  source found here says the bait gathers along those shorelines; it is not shown as an on-screen fact.
- Background (caption only): the lake's giant rainbows are reported to descend from farmed trout that escaped a fish
  farm (Outdoor Canada, per search excerpt; result pages https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3829 and
  https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3828; the excerpt did not say which). Not used on screen.
- Pins "baitfish school", "rocky shoreline", "giant rainbow": labels of the drawn diagram. The baitfish are generic
  cisco-like fish. Depths and slopes are illustrative and not to scale.

## Beat 3: the lure, badge [2] THE JOINTED CRANKBAIT
- "Konrad's record ate a jointed crankbait that looks like a baitfish, worked stop and go" / "A jointed bait, worked
  stop and go." / "FIELD AND STREAM: KONRAD'S RECORD": Field and Stream says the fish took a 5¼ inch, bonito colored
  Rapala X-Rap Jointed that Konrad worked stop and go while back trolling.
  https://www.fieldandstream.com/photos/gallery/fishing/trout-fishing/where-fish-trout/2009/09/new-pending-all-tackle-world-record-ra
  The crankbait drawn is generic (no brand shown), about the same length.
- "Anglers troll baits like it on braided line with a fluorocarbon leader" / "Braided line, fluorocarbon leader." /
  "ANGLER TECHNIQUE · IN FISHERMAN" / badge line "braid, fluorocarbon, baitfish colors": In Fisherman recommends
  trolling big baits 80 to 150 ft back on 30 to 40 lb braid with 25 lb fluorocarbon leaders; a Hooked Magazine piece
  says the lake's clear water makes a fluorocarbon leader a must (it suggests a much lighter leader, 4 to 8 lb);
  Outdoor Life says Konrad used 14 lb braid. Line weights differ between sources, so the film gives none.
  https://in-fisherman.com/editorial/saskatchewan-fishing-land-of-giants/153464
  https://www.outdoorlife.com/fishing/biggest-trout-ever-caught/
- "baitfish colors": the record lure's bonito finish (Field and Stream, above) is a baitfish style pattern.
- Pins "jointed body", "diving lip", "fluorocarbon leader", "braided line": labels of the simulated lure and line.

## Beat 4: the strike, badge [3] STEADY PRESSURE
- "When a giant hits, don't yank" / "Keep the rod bent and the pressure steady" / "ANGLER TECHNIQUE": fight advice
  for big trout: keep steady pressure, avoid sudden jerks that break lines (Biology Insights), keep the rod tip low
  and against the run (KSL). Sources disagree on the hook set itself: the National Park Service says you can set the
  hook from a trolling rod with a sharp upward pull. The film gives fight advice, not a hook set rule.
  https://www.montanaangler.com/montana-fly-fishing-blog/201811/how-to-fight-big-fish
  https://biologyinsights.com/how-to-fight-a-fish-pump-reel-and-drag-tips/
  https://www.ksl.com/article/44702371/5-tips-for-landing-big-fish-on-a-fly-rod
  https://home.nps.gov/articles/trolling.htm
- "set your drag before you fish": Take Me Fishing says to set the drag before your first cast of the day, because
  adjusting it during a fight is hard and can damage some reels.
  https://www.takemefishing.org/how-to-fish/how-to-catch-fish/how-to-set-the-drag
- "let it take the first run" / badge line "smooth drag, let it take the run" / pin "drag giving line": Montana
  Angler says to let the reel's drag take over once the trout has pulled out the working line, and to add pressure
  only after the first run.
  https://www.montanaangler.com/montana-fly-fishing-blog/201811/how-to-fight-big-fish
- Pins "the strike", "rod bent": labels of the simulated scene.

## Outro
- "Find the bait, keep it steady, and you'll see how...": script wording summarising the beats above, running back
  into the opening line (the loop).

## What is simulated
The whole film is computer generated: the rainbow trout, the baitfish, the rocks, the lake bed and its slope, the cut
away diagram, the crankbait, line and leader, the boat, rod and reel, the strike, the bubbles and the light. It is
not footage of Konrad's fish or of any real catch. The voice is a synthetic text to speech voice.

## Changed from the brief
- "an angler landed a mind blowing 48 pound rainbow trout in Canada, a world record that shocked the fishing world":
  rewritten with the angler, lake and IGFA listing; "shocked the fishing world" has no source and was dropped.
- "Giant trout don't stay in tiny shallow streams" and "lock onto deep drop off ledges where baitfish gather": no
  source found for drop off ledges at Lake Diefenbaker; rewritten to baitfish schools and rocky shorelines (Hooked
  Magazine). The cross section therefore shows a rocky shoreline and slope, not a 25 ft ledge.
- "jointed crankbaits or swimbaits ... troll them along deep channel edges": rewritten to Konrad's documented
  jointed crankbait worked stop and go, with braid and a fluorocarbon leader; "deep channel edges" had no source.
- "don't jerk the rod instantly ... set your drag smooth, and let the rod handle the fish's runs": rewritten as
  "don't yank ... set your drag before you fish, and let it take the first run" (Take Me Fishing, Montana Angler).
- "Fish the deep ledges, stay patient": rewritten as "Find the bait, keep it steady" to match the sourced beats.
- The film runs about 41 s (paced to the voiceover) instead of 45 s.

## Caption extras

  given as advice, not as a measured claim.
