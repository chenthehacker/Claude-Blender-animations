The world record rainbow trout:
• Sean Konrad caught a 48 pound rainbow trout on September 5, 2009, on Lake Diefenbaker, Saskatchewan. It measured 42 inches long with a 32 inch girth (Outdoor Life).
• It is reported as the IGFA all tackle world record rainbow trout (Outdoor Life, Field and Stream).
• The lake's giant rainbows are reported to descend from farmed trout that escaped a fish farm (Outdoor Canada).

Where giant rainbow trout hunt:
• Big Diefenbaker rainbows push schools of cisco, their baitfish, to the surface (Hooked Magazine).
• Hooked Magazine suggests starting with large sections of rocky shoreline and covering water by trolling.

The jointed crankbait and line (angler technique, not research):
• Konrad's record took a 5¼ inch, bonito colored jointed crankbait, worked stop and go while back trolling (Field and Stream).
• In Fisherman recommends trolling big baits 80 to 150 feet back on 30 to 40 pound braid with a 25 pound fluorocarbon leader. Other sources use lighter line: Outdoor Life says Konrad used 14 pound braid.

Steady pressure on the strike (angler technique, not research):
• When a giant hits, don't yank. Keep the rod bent and the pressure steady.
• Set your drag before your first cast (Take Me Fishing), and let the drag take the first run before you add pressure (Montana Angler).
• Sources differ on the hook set: the National Park Service says you can set a trolling hook with a sharp upward pull.

Release practice (angler practice): support the trout's body, keep it in the water as much as you can, and check the limits and rules for the water you fish.
