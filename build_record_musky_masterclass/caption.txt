The world record musky:
• Cal Johnson caught a 67 lb 8 oz muskellunge on July 24, 1949, on Lac Courte Oreilles near Hayward, Wisconsin. The IGFA lists it as the all tackle world record (Field and Stream).
• The record is disputed: the Freshwater Fishing Hall of Fame recognizes Louie Spray's 69 lb 11 oz musky from the same year instead, which the IGFA excluded because it was shot beside the boat (Great Lakes Now).
• The Wisconsin DNR calls the musky the "fish of 10,000 casts."

Where giant muskies hunt:
• Muskies hunt mainly by sight and lurk near drop offs, weed edges and weedy bays (Wisconsin DNR).
• They do well in lakes with a wide range of depths and big beds of pondweed, the "cabbage" anglers fish (Wisconsin DNR).
• In northern Wisconsin lakes, yellow perch and white suckers make up most of their diet (Wisconsin DNR study; North American Journal of Fisheries Management, 1999).

Burning bucktails over the weeds (angler technique, not research):
• Burn a double blade bucktail fast, just inches over the weed tops, to trigger a reaction strike from fish hiding in the cabbage.
• Double blades give the bait lift to run over tall weeds.

The figure 8 at the boat (angler technique, not research):
• Never pull your lure out early: finish every cast with a wide, deep figure 8 beside the boat.
• Pro Jim Saric estimates that as many as 40 percent of the muskies he catches hit at the boat during the figure 8 (Mercury Marine). That is one angler's estimate, not a study.

Release practice (angler practice): support the fish's body, keep it in the water as much as you can, and check the musky size limits and rules where you fish.
