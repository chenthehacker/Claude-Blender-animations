# Sources for the record musky masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the voiceover and caption, with where it comes from. Lines marked "angler
technique" are common angling advice, not research. The musky (modelled about 1.4 m with the tail, illustrative and
not the record fish to scale), the lake, its depths and the drop off (the cross section is not to scale), the
bucktail, the boat, the strikes and the figure 8 are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) on 12 November 2026; the notes
paraphrase those excerpts. Lines of the brief's script that the sources did not support were rewritten (see
"Changed from the brief" at the end).

## Beat 1: the record (hook)
- "In 1949, Cal Johnson pulled a 67 pound, 8 ounce musky out of a Wisconsin lake" / "In 1949, Cal Johnson landed a
  67 lb 8 oz musky" / "IGFA ALL TACKLE RECORD · JULY 24, 1949": Field and Stream and other sources report Cal
  Johnson's muskellunge of 67 lb 8 oz, caught on 24 July 1949 on Lac Courte Oreilles near Hayward, Wisconsin, and
  recognised by the IGFA as the all tackle record; its skin mount hangs in Hayward.
  https://www.fieldandstream.com/fishing/the-biggest-world-record-muskies-ever-caught
  https://www.wired2fish.com/fish-biology/the-world-record-musky-biggest-muskies-ever-caught
- "The IGFA lists it as the all tackle world record" / "IGFA RECORDS · FIELD AND STREAM": the same sources; the
  record is disputed. The National Freshwater Fishing Hall of Fame recognises Louie Spray's 69 lb 11 oz musky
  (also 1949, about three months after Johnson's) instead, which the IGFA excluded because it was shot beside the boat, against
  IGFA rules; the World Record Muskie Alliance challenged Johnson's fish, and the IGFA rejected that challenge.
  https://www.greatlakesnow.org/?p=28174
  https://www.themeateater.com/fish/freshwater/mobsters-arson-and-photogrammetry-the-world-record-muskie-conspiracy
  https://www.outdoorsfirst.com/muskie/article/wrma-johnson-summary-report/
  No 2024 to 2026 IGFA statement was found, so the record's status in November 2026 was not confirmed against the
  IGFA records search.
- "the fish of ten thousand casts": the Wisconsin DNR's musky page uses this nickname for the musky (a nickname, not
  a measured catch rate). https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/Fishing/musky/muskywaters_fishing.html
- Pin "muskellunge": label of the simulated fish.

## Beat 2: the structure, badge [1] CABBAGE WEED EDGE
- "Big muskies hunt by sight from cover" / "WISCONSIN DNR": the Wisconsin DNR fact sheet says muskies rely mainly on
  sight to catch prey, and that they lurk near drop offs, rock or sand bars, weed edges, weed covered bays and shady
  shoreline. https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/sites/default/files/topic/Fishing/Musky_MuskellungeFactSheet2012.pdf
- "They lurk along cabbage weed edges, right next to deep water drop offs" / "WISCONSIN DNR MUSKY FACT SHEET": the
  same fact sheet says muskies do well in lakes with a wide range of depths and large beds of aquatic plants such as
  pondweeds ("cabbage"). That the edges next to deep water are a classic place to find them is angler advice
  (e.g. "clear water + abundant cabbage weed + adjacent deep water drop off").
- Pins "cabbage weed edge", "drop off", "musky in ambush": labels of the drawn diagram. Depths and slopes are
  illustrative and not to scale.
- The perch: yellow perch and white suckers were the main food items of muskellunge in a study of northern
  Wisconsin lakes (North American Journal of Fisheries Management, 1999;
  https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1577/1548-8675%281999%29019%3C0258%3ADOMINW%3E2.0.CO%3B2), and a Wisconsin DNR study of 1,092 musky stomachs found mostly perch and white suckers.
  https://www.pineconepresscitizen.com/news/2021/jun/10/studies-reveal-what-muskies-eat-impacts-on-other-f/
  The brief's ducklings are not shown: no source found in this search named them as musky prey.

## Beat 3: the lure, badge [2] BURN THE BUCKTAIL
- "To trigger a reaction strike, anglers burn double blade bucktails fast, just inches over the weed tops, where
  muskies hide below" / "ANGLER TECHNIQUE": a musky retailer describes burning bucktails rapidly just inches above
  the weed tops on shallow weed flats; Mepps says its double blade design gives lift to run above high weed growth;
  a guide describes lifting the rod to run a bucktail over weed tops to trigger fish hiding in the weeds.
  https://www.muskyshop.com/blogs/news/northwoods-awakening-top-5-bucktails-to-ignite-your-early-summer-musky-season
  https://www.mepps.com/information/double-blade-musky-flashabou/900
  "Reaction strike" is common angler usage; no controlled study was found.
- Pins "double blades", "hair skirt", "inches over the weeds", "musky below": labels of the simulated lure and fish.
  The bucktail drawn (about 26 cm, two blades) is generic.

## Beat 4: the figure 8, badge [3] THE FIGURE 8 FINISH
- "Never pull your lure out early" / "finish every cast with a wide, deep figure eight" (angler technique): the figure
  8 at the boat is standard musky technique (Mercury Marine, Bass Pro Shops).
- "Some pros say up to forty percent of their muskies hit right at the boat" / "Up to 40 percent hit at the boat" /
  "PRO ESTIMATE: JIM SARIC, MERCURY MARINE": Mercury Pro Team member Jim Saric estimates that as many as 40 percent of
  the muskies he catches hit at the side of the boat during the figure 8; a Bass Pro Shops article gives 35 to 40
  percent for another angler. These are personal estimates, not a study; no study was found.
  https://www.mercurymarine.com/us/en/lifestyle/dockline/how-to-catch-more-muskies-using-the-figure-8-technique
  https://1source.basspro.com/news-tips/muskie/6274/catch-more-muskie-casting-easy-figure-8-maneuver-video
- Pins "wide, deep figure 8", "eats on the turn": labels of the simulated scene.

## Outro
- "Burn fast, work the turn, and you'll understand why...": script wording summarising the beats above, running back
  into the opening line (the loop).

## What is simulated
The whole film is computer generated: the musky, the perch, the cabbage and lily pads, the lake bed and the drop off,
the cut away diagram, the bucktail, the boat and rod tip, the strikes, the bubbles and the light. It is not footage
of Johnson's fish or of any real catch.

## Changed from the brief
- "world record 67 pound 8 ounce musky": kept, with "The IGFA lists it as" because the record is disputed.
- "downsizing won't work" and "lockjaw": no source found; dropped.
- "Over 50% of record muskies eat at the boat": no source found; the pros' estimate is up to about 40 percent of
  their own fish, so the line says that.
- "aren't roaming deep open water aimlessly": rewritten as "hunt by sight from cover" (Wisconsin DNR).
- Ducklings as prey: not shown (not confirmed); perch are.
- 10 inch glide baits: not shown; the lure beat covers the double blade bucktail the voiceover names.
- The lake's name is spelled Lac Courte Oreilles.
- The film runs about 43 s (paced to the voiceover) instead of 45 s.

## Caption extras
- "support the fish's body, keep it in the water as much as you can" (angler practice): common catch and release
  advice, given as advice, not as a measured claim.
- "How many casts did your first musky take?": a comment prompt, not a claim.
