Why do most anglers never land a 10 pound bass? Usually because they fish where the small ones live. Here is how trophy hunters find and fool the big females.

Where the giants live:
• Trophy bass are females. A Texas Parks and Wildlife biologist says male bass typically do not grow that large.
• Big bass relate to deep structure: Texas Parks and Wildlife notes bass suspended in timber and along drop offs in summer, at or above the thermocline (often around 15 feet), and stacked in the creek channel in winter.
• They do use the shallows too. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife reports bass moving from drop offs onto flats as the water warms, so treat deep structure as a starting point, not a law.
• Search ledges, creek channel bends and submerged timber in roughly 15 to 30 feet of water (angler technique, not research).

The magnum presentation (angler technique, not research):
• Big bass eat big prey. Shad and sunfish are principal prey in southeastern reservoirs (SEAFWA), and in Twin Lakes, Washington, large bass ate stocked rainbow trout (about 6.3 percent of the fall stocking in one lake).
• Throw 8 to 10 inch swimbaits or glide baits and fish them slowly through the strike zone.

The strike:
• Largemouth bass are suction feeders: they expand the mouth and draw water and prey in (Journal of Experimental Biology). It often feels like weight, not a tap.
• Heavy power rods and 20 lb fluorocarbon give you the backbone to turn a big fish away from timber (angler technique).

Handle trophies with care: support the belly, keep her in the water as much as you can, and release her quickly.

Go big. Fish deep.
