# Sources for the record bass masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the caption, with where it comes from. Things marked
"angler technique" are common angling advice, not research. The 15+ lb fish, the depths in the scene,
the distances and the strike itself are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the agency pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) on 20 October 2026;
direct page fetches from this build machine were blocked, so quotes are paraphrased from those excerpts.

## Hook
- "Why *most* anglers never land a *10 pound* bass." The script's original line said "99% of anglers". No
  source for a 99% figure was found, so the video says "most". Supporting context: the Florida Fish and
  Wildlife Conservation Commission, quoted by Florida Sportsman, says only a small percentage of bass live
  long enough (at least 10 years) to reach 10 pounds, and the average Florida bass lives about 5 to 6 years.
  https://floridasportsman.com/editorial/top-5-florida-lakes-big-bass/396885
- "Because you're fishing where small fish live." Angler technique (rhetorical framing, not a measured claim).

## Beat 1: deep structure
- "Record bass are a different animal" / on-screen source line "trophy bass are females": a Texas Parks and
  Wildlife biologist, on the Toyota ShareLunker program, says male bass typically don't get that large, so
  trophy fish are normally all female. Houston Chronicle coverage makes the same point.
  https://www.wired2fish.com/news/big-changes-coming-to-toyota-sharelunker-program-in-2018
  https://passporttotexas.org/category/shows/fishing/sharelunker-program/page/2/
- "They hold on deep structure" (source: Texas Parks and Wildlife): for Muenster Lake, TPWD says bass may be
  caught suspended in timber or along drop-offs in summer, at or above the thermocline, which usually sets
  up at about 15 feet; in winter they stack up in the creek channel in the deepest water.
  https://tpwd.texas.gov/fishboat/fish/recreational/lakes/muenster/
- Caveat (not shown on screen, stated in the caption): bass do use shallow water. Kentucky Fish and Wildlife
  says bass at Cedar Creek Lake hold near drop-offs and the old creek channel in winter and early spring,
  then move onto flats as the water warms. https://app.fw.ky.gov/kfwis/destinations/Cedar_Creek_Lake_WMA.htm
- "Ledges, channel bends, submerged timber" and the badge "15 to 30 ft of water": the structure types match
  the TPWD and Kentucky pages above; the 15 to 30 ft range is angler guidance from the script (TPWD's
  thermocline figure supports only the shallow end). The on-screen source line says so.

## Beat 2: magnum presentation
- "Stop throwing small baits." Angler technique.
- "Trout, shad, bluegill: big prey" (sources: SEAFWA, Idaho Fish and Game):
  - Shad and sunfish (bluegill is a sunfish) are principal prey of largemouth bass in southeastern
    reservoirs: Miranda, L. E., proceedings of the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies,
    pages 153 to 163. https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/MIRANDA-153-163.pdf
  - Rainbow trout: in Twin Lakes, Washington, large largemouth bass (300 mm and longer) ate stocked trout;
    about 6.3% of the fall stocked rainbow trout in South Twin Lake were eaten by bass, and bass predation did
    not explain the overall trout losses. Reported in Idaho Department of Fish and Game, Regional Fisheries
    Management Investigations, Southeast Region (Teuscher, 2002).
    https://collaboration.idfg.idaho.gov/FisheriesTechnicalReports/Mgt03-06Teuscher2002%20Regional%20Fisheries%20Management%20Investigations%20Southeast%20Region.pdf
  - "High calorie" is the script's wording; no calorie comparison was sourced, so it is not shown on screen.
- "8 to 10 inch baits, fished ultra slow" and the "8 INCHES" dimension: angler technique. The modelled
  swimbait is 8 inches (0.2032 m) long; it is a generic three section jointed trout swimbait, not a brand.

## Beat 3: the strike and the tackle
- "It's a heavy suction" (source: Journal of Experimental Biology): largemouth bass are suction feeders;
  Higham, Day and Wainwright (2006) measured the flow of water into the mouths of largemouth bass and
  bluegill, and a later synthesis (Wainwright et al., Integrative and Comparative Biology, 2007) reports that
  bass draw a larger peak flux of water into the mouth than bluegill, while bluegill are more accurate.
  Higham, T. E., Day, S. W. and Wainwright, P. C. (2006). Multidimensional analysis of suction feeding
  performance in fishes: fluid speed, acceleration, strike accuracy and the ingested volume of water.
  Journal of Experimental Biology 209, 2713 to 2725. doi:10.1242/jeb.02315
- The slow motion strike, the debris drawn into the mouth and the jaw motion are an illustration, not a
  measured reconstruction.
- "Heavy power rod, 20 lb fluorocarbon": angler technique. The modelled rod is a generic 7 ft 6 in blank.
