# Sources for the on-screen text

Every factual line in the video is listed here with where it comes from. Lines marked "technique"
describe how anglers fish, not research findings.

How this was checked: the official pages (adfg.alaska.gov, nps.gov, fws.gov) could not be opened from
the build machine (its network policy blocks them), so the wording below was confirmed from the text of
those pages as quoted in web search results on 15 October 2026. Regulations change every season: the video
tells viewers to check current local regulations, and so should anyone reusing these lines.

## Hook and outro

**"Stop wasting your day: sockeye pass behind you."** and **"They're passing right behind you."**
Alaska Department of Fish and Game (ADF&G), Russian River fishing guide (RussianRiver.pdf, Southcentral
Region sport fish publications): "Sockeye salmon travel very close to shore, and inexperienced anglers
often wade or cast too far out, not realizing the fish are probably passing behind them."
https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static-sf/Region2/pdfpubs/RussianRiver.pdf

The brief asked for "Why 90% of anglers miss sockeye". No source for a 90% figure was found, so that number
is not used; the hook above says the same thing with a sourced statement instead.

## Beat 2: the migration highway and the tailout

**"Sockeye travel very close to shore."** (badge [1] subtext and caption): ADF&G, as above; the same guide
adds "Since this species travels close to shore, most sockeye salmon fishing takes place from the bank."

**"They hold in slower water before running the riffles."** ADF&G Russian River guide: "The most
productive fishing areas are pools immediately downstream of shallow riffles, and mainstem close to shore.
Sockeye salmon tend to hold in these areas before migrating through the riffles." The caption shortens
this. Badge [2] "THE TAILOUT ZONE" names the place where the animated school holds (the slow tail of the
pool, just above the next riffle); "tailout" is the brief's word, not ADF&G's.

**"Wade in too far and they slip behind you."** ADF&G, the "passing behind them" quote above.

## Beat 3: the drift rig (technique)

**"Flip it 10 to 15 feet upstream, about 45 degrees."** and **"Let it dead drift with the current."**
ADF&G Russian River guide: "Most anglers flip their fly 10 to 15 feet upstream into the current at about a
45-degree angle, and allow the fly to dead drift with the current until reaching its furthest downstream
point. It is then retrieved and the procedure repeated."

**"Add weight so the fly bounces the bottom."** ADF&G Russian River guide: "Enough weight must be used so
that the fly bounces very near the bottom."

**"Weight at least 18 inches ahead of the fly."** (and the "18 IN +" dimension): ADF&G Russian River
regulations summary: "Legal tackle in the Russian River and Sanctuary area is one unweighted, single-hook,
artificial fly, with gap between point and shank 3/8 inch or less. If weights are used, they must be at
least 18 inches ahead of the fly." This is a Russian River (Alaska) rule, labelled as such on screen. The
animated leader is 0.55 m (21.7 in); the animated hook gap is about 9 mm (0.35 in, inside 3/8 in).

**Pink and orange yarn fly**: the fly pattern the brief asked for. ADF&G names the Russian River Coho
streamer, Green Lantern, Comet and shrimp patterns as common choices; no claim is made on screen that pink
or orange works better. "Strike zone" marks where the animated fly rides, just off the bottom at the
holding fishes' mouth height (illustration, not a measured value).

## Beat 4: the strike and the hook-set

**"Sockeye stop feeding once they reach fresh water."** and **"But they will sometimes strike."**
National Park Service, Katmai National Park and Preserve, Sockeye Salmon Frequently Asked Questions:
"Since salmon do not feed once they reenter fresh water they are difficult to catch, but they will
sometimes strike at lures."
https://www.nps.gov/katm/learn/photosmultimedia/sockeye-salmon-frequently-asked-questions.htm

**"Keep only fish hooked in the mouth."** and **"Hooked anywhere else? Release it right away."**
ADF&G Reel Times newsletter: "Remember it is illegal to keep snagged fish, the only legally hooked fish is
when the fish is hooked in the mouth." ADF&G statewide regulations: a fish unintentionally hooked
elsewhere than the mouth must be released immediately; "snag" means to hook a fish elsewhere than in the
mouth. https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=FishingSportFishAK.reelTimes&ID=30
These are Alaska rules; elsewhere the rules differ, hence "CHECK CURRENT LOCAL REGULATIONS" at the end.

Badge [4] subtext "they don't feed, but they do strike" paraphrases the NPS sentence above. Badge [5]
subtext "keep only mouth-hooked fish" paraphrases the ADF&G rule above.

## The sockeye

Spawning colours (bright red body, green head and tail) as described by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service
sockeye fact sheet ("their heads turn green and their bodies turn bright red") and Olympic National Park.
Size: USFWS gives an average length of 18 to 31 inches; the animated fish are about 0.62 m (24 in), with
scale variations of plus or minus 10 percent. The model is an AI generated mesh (see assets/CREDITS.md),
checked by eye against those descriptions; it shows a hooked jaw and a slight hump, which were not
confirmed in the sources above.

## What is simulated

The river, banks, forest, mountains, fish, angler and tackle are all computer generated. The river layout
(riffle, pool, tailout), depths, current speeds, distances and the school's size are illustrative values
chosen for the animation, not measurements of a real river. The weight and the fly are drawn at 1.5 times
real size so they read on a phone screen (the 0.55 m leader between them is not scaled). The turquoise colour is an artistic choice:
the National Park Service notes that glacial flour often makes glacier-fed rivers grey or milky, with
turquoise more typical of glacial lakes.
