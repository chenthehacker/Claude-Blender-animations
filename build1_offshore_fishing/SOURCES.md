# Sources for the on-screen text

Every factual line in the video is listed here with where it comes from. Lines marked
"technique" describe how anglers fish, not research findings. Citation details and abstracts were
checked through web searches; the full papers were not read.

## Beat A: the weed line

**"Many mahi gather near floating weed and drifting objects."**
NOAA Fisheries, Atlantic Mahi Mahi species page: females and smaller males are commonly found near
natural and artificial floating objects, including floating Sargassum.
https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/atlantic-mahi-mahi

**"In a Gulf Stream survey, weed held far more small fish than open water."**
Casazza, T.L. & Ross, S.W. (2008). Fishes associated with pelagic Sargassum and open water lacking
Sargassum in the Gulf Stream off North Carolina. Fishery Bulletin 106(4): 348 to 363.
Sargassum samples held significantly more fish (18,799, at least 80 species) than open water samples
(2,706, 60 species); most were juveniles.

**Pin "shade under the mat"**: a floating mat blocks sunlight from above (illustration of the scene).

## Beat B: the spread and the school

**"Two baits ride wide on the outriggers, two close on the flat lines."** and
**"They skip on top and dive, trailing bubbles."**: technique, describing the spread as animated.
Outriggers spreading lines wide: The Tackle Room, How to Set Up Outriggers for Trolling.
https://thetackleroom.com/blogs/news/how-to-set-up-outriggers-for-trolling

**"Mahi feed at the surface on fish and crabs from the weed."**
Manooch, C.S., Mason, D.L. & Nelson, R.S. (1984). Food and gastrointestinal parasites of dolphin
Coryphaena hippurus collected along the southeastern and Gulf coasts of the United States.
Bulletin of the Japanese Society of Scientific Fisheries 50(9): 1511 to 1525. From 2,632 stomachs:
dolphin frequently feed at the surface on fishes, crustaceans, insects and plants associated with
floating Sargassum. Swimming crabs are also named as dolphinfish prey in Rudershausen, P.J., Buckel,
J.A., Edwards, J., Gannon, D.P., Butler, C.M. & Averett, T.W. (2010). Feeding Ecology of Blue Marlins,
Dolphinfish, Yellowfin Tuna, and Wahoos from the North Atlantic Ocean and Comparisons with Other
Oceans. Transactions of the American Fisheries Society.

## Beat C: the strike and the drag

**"When a fish strikes, the line pops free of the release clip."**: technique.
The Tackle Room (link above): when a fish strikes, the line pops free from the release clip and you
fight directly off the rod tip.

**"That moment of slack lets the fish turn the bait."**: technique.
Florida Sportsman, No-Rigger Trolling Tips (22 May 2018): a little slack gives the fish time to grab
the bait and turn it before swallowing; dolphin are named among fish that take prey whole.
https://floridasportsman.com/editorial/no-rigger-trolling-tips/398836

**"Strike drag: about 25% of line strength on 30 to 50 lb mono."** and the gauge's 20 to 30% band:
technique. Salt Water Sportsman / George Poveromo, How to Manage Your Drag: 20 percent for mono up to
20 lb test, 25 percent for 30 to 50 lb mono, 30 percent for 80 to 130 lb mono; set it with a scale.
https://saltwatersportsman.com/how-to-manage-your-drag

## Beat D: the release

**"Unhook it in the water whenever you can."**, **"If it comes out, keep it under 60 seconds."**,
**"Wet hands, a soft net, a dehooker ready."**
NOAA Fisheries, Catch and Release Fishing Best Practices: dehook in the water when possible; if a fish
must come out, aim for less than 60 seconds; handle as little as possible with wet hands; use a wet,
soft knotless or rubber net; release tools such as dehookers.
https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/resources-fishing/catch-and-release-fishing-best-practices

**"Check your local limits."**: bag and size limits differ by region and change; no number is given.

## The mahi model

Procedural, built from published descriptions of the species rather than from a downloaded model:
a single dorsal fin from the head almost to the tail, a concave anal fin from mid body almost to the
tail, a deeply forked tail, and in adult males a steep, near vertical forehead with a bony crest
(FishBase, Coryphaena hippurus morphology, https://fishbase.se/physiology/Coryphaena_hippurus, and the
Coryphaenidae family summary, https://fishbase.ca/summary/FamilySummary.php?ID=315). Body depth of
roughly a quarter of standard length, small dark spots on the sides and a dark green to deep blue
dorsal fin (Fishes of Australia, https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/1730). Golden green and
blue on top fading to a yellow white underside, with metallic blue and green patches (Florida Museum,
https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/dolphinfish).

## What is simulated

The boat, crew, fish, baits, weed line and sea are procedural models. Depths, distances and speeds on
screen are illustrative; the depth gauge shows the camera's own depth. The boat trolls at about
6 knots (3.1 m/s), a value chosen for the animation, not a recommendation.
