The world record Pacific blue marlin:
• Jay de Beaubien caught a 1,376 pound Pacific blue marlin on May 31, 1982, off Kona, Hawaii, aboard No Problem with Capt. Bobby Brown. It is listed as the IGFA all tackle record for Pacific blue marlin (Marlin Magazine, Outdoor Life).
• The record fish measured 193 inches long with an 82 inch girth, and it ate a silver and blue Kita lure (Marlin Magazine).
• It was landed in under an hour; Marlin Magazine gives about 40 minutes in one account.

Where blue marlin hunt off Kona:
• Kona guides describe currents pushing onto the underwater ledges, gathering baitfish and drawing marlin and tuna (FishTrack).
• A study of 87 blue marlin caught in Hawaiian waters found small tunas were the single most important part of their diet (Brock 1984, Pacific Science).

The marlin trolling spread (angler technique, not research):
• Run a staggered spread of four lures: short corner, long corner, short rigger and long rigger.
• Troll big skirted resin lures at about 7 to 9 knots.
• Lures that pop and smoke, leaving a trail of bubbles, are the classic marlin lure action.

Strike drag for big marlin (angler technique, not research):
• A common rule of thumb is a strike drag near 30 percent of the line's breaking strength, nearly 40 pounds on 130 pound line (Marlin Magazine).
• Check your drag with a scale before you fish.

If you release a blue marlin, keep it in the water and revive it before you let it go (angler practice), and check the fishing rules where you fish.
