The world record bluefin tuna:
• Ken Fraser caught a 1,496 pound Atlantic bluefin tuna on October 26, 1979, off Aulds Cove, Nova Scotia. It is still listed as the IGFA all tackle world record (Outdoor Life, Saltwater Sportsman).
• The record fish measured 128.5 inches long with a 99 inch girth (Outdoor Life).
• He fished a Penn International 14/0 reel with 130 pound line, trolling a mackerel (Outdoor Life).

Giant bluefin tuna tackle (angler technique, not research):
• 130 pound class monofilament on a 14/0 class reel, a roller guide rod and a fighting chair.
• Troll fresh mackerel or herring, and work temperature breaks where warm and cold water meet.

Where giant bluefin tuna swim:
• Tagging of 561 Atlantic bluefin found their mean diving depth tracked the depth of the thermocline, the boundary between warm surface water and colder water below (Walli et al. 2009, PLoS ONE).
• Archival tags have recorded Atlantic bluefin diving below 1,000 meters, about 3,281 feet, while keeping a warm body (Block et al. 2001, Science).

How to fight a giant bluefin from the chair (angler technique, not research):
• When a giant runs, the captain backs the boat down hard so the angler can keep line on the spool.
• Drag advice varies: a common strike setting is about 25 to 30 percent of the line's breaking strength (roughly 30 to 40 pounds on 130 pound line), with more drag for the fight on very large fish.

Ken Fraser died in October 2025, aged 81 (Marlin Magazine). His bluefin tuna world record still stands.
