# Sources for the flathead catfish masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the caption, with where it comes from. Lines marked "angler technique"
are common angling advice, not research. The 40+ lb fish, the 20 ft depth of the scour hole, the distances, the
glowing eyes (a stylised eyeshine) and the strike itself are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the agency pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) on 22 October 2026;
direct page fetches from this build machine were blocked, so quotes are paraphrased from those excerpts.

## Hook
- "Stop throwing dead stink bait on the bottom for flatheads" / "You're catching channel cats, not river
  monsters": Texas Parks and Wildlife says other catfish are scavengers while flatheads prey only on live fish;
  South Carolina DNR says flatheads are reluctant to take decaying bait and prefer live bait.
  https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/species/catfish
  https://www.dnr.sc.gov/fish/species/flatheadcatfish.html
  "River monsters" is rhetoric, not a claim.

## Beat 1: apex ambush lair
- "Flatheads aren't scavengers. They're apex predators." (source line: live prey only, Texas Parks and Wildlife):
  TPWD says flatheads of 10 inches and larger eat only fish (shad, carp, suckers, sunfish, largemouth bass and
  other catfish). Michigan DNR calls adults ambush predators that feed only on live prey (it also notes an
  introduced population that ate mostly crayfish). West Virginia DNR says large flatheads prey primarily on live
  fish and crayfish. "Apex" is the script's word, not an agency classification.
  https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/flathead-catfish
  https://wvdnr.gov/plants-animals/sportfish/flathead-catfish/
- "Solitary" (voiceover): a Mississippi study in the SEAFWA proceedings (Skains) reports flatheads are usually
  solitary and aggressive to their own species, so one unit of cover usually holds one, or at most two or three,
  adults. Florida FWC also describes a solitary lifestyle.
  https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/SKAINS-539-546.pdf
  https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/profiles/freshwater/flathead-catfish/
- "By day they hide in heavy cover" (source: Minnesota DNR): the flathead occupies deep pools with dense cover
  such as log jams. https://dnr.state.mn.us/fish/catfish/biology.html
- "Root balls, logjams, undercut banks" (source: South Carolina DNR): deep holes in slow water in medium to large
  rivers with structure such as fallen trees, stumps and undercut banks.
- "20 FT SCOUR HOLE" pin: the depth is illustrative (the modelled hole is 6.1 m); the voiceover says "deep river
  scour holes" without a number.

## Beat 2: live bait only
- "At dusk they leave the wood to hunt" (source: Minnesota DNR): at night the flathead may feed more actively or
  even forage in shallow riffles.
- "Use live, struggling bait": angler technique, consistent with the agency notes above on live prey.
- "6 to 8 inch bluegill, 8/0 circle hook", the slip sinker rig (egg sinker, bead, swivel, leader): angler
  technique. The on-screen line adds "check your local bait rules" because using live bluegill (a game fish in
  many states) as bait is allowed in some states and banned in others; for example, Kansas allows bluegill and
  green sunfish from non-designated waters as live bait, while Minnesota does not allow game fish as bait.
  https://ksoutdoors.gov/outdoor-activities/fishing-in-kansas/fish-bait
- The rings around the kicking bait are a visual aid for vibration, not a measurement.

## Beat 3: structural hold
- "The vibrations draw them out" (source line: lateral line, Minnesota DNR): flatheads hunt using smell and the
  ability to detect vibration through the lateral line. "Drive them wild" (voiceover) is rhetoric.
- "Let the rod load completely" / "A bony mouth needs maximum pressure", the badge "let it load, then lock the
  drag": angler technique. With circle hooks the common advice is to let the fish load the rod rather than jerk a
  hook set; no source was found for the "bony mouth" claim, so it is labelled angler technique.
- The strike (the flathead engulfing the bait) is an illustration. Suction feeding is documented in catfish (for
  example channel catfish, Brown University, 2019), but no flathead specific study was used.

## Caption: handling and release
- "Support the body horizontally, keep her wet, release her quickly": Maryland Department of Natural Resources
  catch and release guidance (written for striped bass; it advises keeping fish in the water when possible,
  holding them horizontally with support and returning them quickly).
  https://dnr.maryland.gov/fisheries/Pages/Catch-and-Release.aspx

## Outro
- Recap and "Ditch the dead bait. Target heavy wood.": summary of the above. The call to action is the channel's.
