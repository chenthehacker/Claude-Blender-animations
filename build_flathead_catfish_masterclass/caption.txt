Stop throwing dead stink bait on the bottom if you want a giant flathead. Here is where the big ones hide and how to pull them out of the wood.

Where flatheads live:
• Flatheads are not scavengers. Texas Parks and Wildlife says flatheads 10 inches and longer eat only live fish, unlike other catfish, and South Carolina DNR says they are reluctant to take decaying bait.
• They live alone. A Mississippi study found one unit of cover usually holds one, or at most two or three, adults.
• By day they hold in deep pools and holes with heavy cover: logjams, fallen trees, stumps and undercut banks (Minnesota DNR, South Carolina DNR).
• At night they feed more actively, and they find prey by smell and by feeling vibration through the lateral line (Minnesota DNR).

The live bait rig (angler technique, not research):
• Rig a lively 6 to 8 inch bluegill or bullhead on an 8/0 circle hook, on a leader below a bead, swivel and sliding egg sinker.
• Fish it close to the wood at dusk and after dark, where a hunting flathead will find it.
• Check your state's rules before using bluegill as bait. Some states allow it, others ban using game fish as bait.

The strike (angler technique, not research):
• When a flathead takes the bait, let the rod load fully instead of jerking a hook set; the circle hook does the work. Then lock down the drag and keep her out of the wood with steady, heavy pressure.

Handle big fish with care: support the body horizontally, keep her wet, and release her quickly if you are not keeping her (Maryland DNR catch and release guidance).

Ditch the dead bait. Target heavy wood.

Sources: Texas Parks and Wildlife, flathead catfish species profile; South Carolina DNR, flathead catfish species page; Michigan DNR, flathead catfish; West Virginia DNR, flathead catfish; Minnesota DNR, catfish biology and identification; Skains, Proceedings of the Southeastern Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies, pages 539 to 546 (Mississippi study of flathead behavior); Florida FWC, flathead catfish profile; Kansas Department of Wildlife and Parks, bait fish FAQ; Maryland Department of Natural Resources, Catch and Release guidance.
