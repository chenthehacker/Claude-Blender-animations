# Sources for the night squid masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the voiceover and caption, with where it comes from. Lines marked "angler
technique" are common angling advice, not research. The squid (modelled on a loliginid squid, mantle 28 cm), the
pier, the lamp and its cone, the baitfish, the plankton sparks (stylised, not photophores), the jig's glow trail
(a drawn path), the depths and the cross section (not to scale) are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) on 25 October 2026; the notes
paraphrase those excerpts. Three lines of the original script were changed because no source supported them:
"squid see UV light far better than white light" (the evidence says blue green), and two "90 percent" figures
(no source found for either number).

## Beat 1: the hook
- "Why most anglers fish blindly for squid" / "Stop casting blindly into the dark": script rhetoric, not a measured
  claim. No percentage is given (none was found in any source).
- "Squid hunt with their eyes" / "how their eyes hunt": squid are visual predators with large camera eyes; the
  review below covers cephalopod eyes and visual pigments.
  Review of cephalopod visual pigments (PubMed Central PMC5031660; authors not recorded from the excerpt).
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5031660/
- The skin pattern on the macro (chromatophores opening and closing) is a stylised animation of a real mechanism:
  loliginid and bigfin reef squid change colour with chromatophore organs.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8960755/

## Beat 2: the light cone, badge [1] THE LIGHT CONE
- "Squid don't sit in the light. They lurk on the shadow line" / "CONSISTENT WITH JIGGING BOAT LIGHT STUDIES" /
  pins "light cone", "shadow line": a Korean tank study and a Japanese study of squid jigging boat lamps both
  found squid concentrating in the shaded zone at the edge of the lit area. Both studies concern tanks and fishing
  vessels, not docks, so the film says "consistent with", not "proven at docks".
  https://db.koreascholar.com/Article/Detail/256719
  Nippon Suisan Gakkaishi 78(6), jigging boat light study (English abstract):
  https://www.miyagi.kopas.co.jp/JSFS/jsfs-english/E-PUB/78-6/78_1104.html
- "Light draws baitfish. Squid ambush from the dark" / "High power green or white lights draw in small baitfish"
  (angler technique): standard dock and boat light advice for squid; the eging article below describes fishing
  lit water at night. https://www.thefisherman.com/article/plugging-for-squid-the-art-of-eging/
- Pins "baitfish in the light", "squid wait in the dark": labels of the simulated scene. The cone angle (46 degrees)
  and the green colour of the lamp are illustrative, not a product specification.

## Beat 3: the jig, badge [2] GLOWING EGI JIG
- "A cloth wrapped egi jig" (angler technique) / pins "cloth wrap", "weighted nose", "barbless crown": the egi is a
  Japanese squid jig with a cloth covered body, a weighted nose and a crown of barbless needle hooks. The eging
  articles below cover the egi and its use; the construction details are general angler knowledge rather than a
  quote from them. The model is generic, not a specific brand.
  https://www.thefisherman.com/article/plugging-for-squid-the-art-of-eging/
  https://www.seabreeze.com.au/Articles/Fishing/Southern-Calamari-The-Egi-Technique-That-Works_23377853.aspx
- "Squid eyes are tuned to blue green light" / "CEPHALOPOD VISION: ONE BLUE GREEN PIGMENT": most cephalopods have a
  single visual pigment sensitive to blue green light, peaking around 470 to 505 nm (PMC5031660 above). A pupil
  response study on the brief squid (Lolliguncula brevis) found peak sensitivity at 500 nm (Society for Integrative
  and Comparative Biology abstract). https://sicb.org/?p=23467
- "A glowing silhouette stands out" / "glowing body" pin (angler technique): glow and contrast are common reasons
  given for luminous egi; this specific claim was not tested in a cited study.
- "eggy" in the voiceover script is a phonetic spelling of egi, so the voice reads it correctly.

## Beat 4: hop and fall, badge [3] HOP AND FALL
- "Three sharp rod jerks", "Then pause", "Most strikes come on the fall" / pins "dart up", "the drift down", "strike"
  (angler technique): the standard eging retrieve is a series of sharp upward rod jerks followed by a pause while
  the jig sinks, and the angler sources say most takes come on the fall or pause. No percentage is given (no source
  was found for one).
  https://www.thefisherman.com/article/plugging-for-squid-the-art-of-eging/
  https://www.seabreeze.com.au/Articles/Fishing/Southern-Calamari-The-Egi-Technique-That-Works_23377853.aspx
- The squid's strike (the two long tentacles shooting out, then the arms wrapping the jig) follows how loliginid
  squid catch prey (general description; no specific source is attached). The timing is animated, not measured.

## Outro
- "Master the shadow line, lock in your drift": script wording summarising the beats above.

## What is simulated
The whole film is computer generated: the squid, its colour changes, the pier, the lamp and the light cone, the
water, the baitfish, the plankton sparks (stylised; the film does not claim these squid have light organs), the jig,
its glow trail and the strike. It is not footage of a real catch.

The thumbnail adds a school of glowing baitfish and drifting plankton specks (thumbnail_scene.py): a stylised
illustration, not bioluminescence and not a shot in the film.
