Night squid fishing under dock lights: how to catch squid at night with an egi jig. Where squid hold on the shadow line of a pier light, why a glowing cloth egi stands out, and the eging retrieve that gets most strikes on the fall.

Where squid hold at night:
• Squid gather in the shaded zone at the edge of the lit water rather than in the brightest light. A Korean tank study and a Japanese study of squid jigging boat lamps both found squid concentrating in that shadow zone (Korea Scholar; Nippon Suisan Gakkaishi 78(6)). Those studies were on tanks and fishing boats, not docks.
• Lights draw small baitfish into the lit water, and squid ambush them from the dark edge (angler technique, not research).

Why a glowing egi jig works:
• Most cephalopods have a single visual pigment tuned to blue green light, peaking around 470 to 505 nanometres (review of cephalopod visual pigments, PubMed Central).
• A study of the brief squid, Lolliguncula brevis, found peak sensitivity at 500 nanometres (Society for Integrative and Comparative Biology abstract).
• Squid change colour with chromatophores, tiny pigment organs in the skin that open and close (PubMed Central).

The egi jig (angler technique, not research):
• A cloth wrapped body over a glowing or bright core, a weighted nose that sets the sink angle, and a crown of barbless needle hooks.
• A bright silhouette is easier to see against dark water.

How to work an egi at night (angler technique, not research):
• Give three sharp, vertical rod jerks so the jig darts upward.
• Pause and let it glide down slowly. Most strikes come on the fall.
• Keep the jig working along the shadow line, not in the middle of the light.
