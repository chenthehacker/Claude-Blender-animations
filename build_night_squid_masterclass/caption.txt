How to catch squid at night: night squid fishing under dock lights and pier lights with an egi squid jig. Where squid hold on the shadow line, why a glowing cloth egi gets noticed, and the eging retrieve that gets most squid strikes on the fall.

Where squid hold under dock lights at night:
• Squid gather in the shaded zone at the edge of the lit water, not in the brightest light. A Korean tank study and a Japanese study of squid jigging boat lamps both found squid concentrating in that shadow zone (Korea Scholar; Nippon Suisan Gakkaishi 78(6)). Those studies were on tanks and fishing boats, not docks.
• Dock lights and pier lights draw small baitfish into the lit water, and squid ambush them from the dark edge (angler technique, not research).

Why a glowing egi squid jig works:
• Most cephalopods have a single visual pigment tuned to blue green light, peaking around 470 to 505 nanometres (review of cephalopod visual pigments, PubMed Central).
• A study of the brief squid, Lolliguncula brevis, found peak sensitivity at 500 nanometres (Society for Integrative and Comparative Biology abstract).
• Squid change colour with chromatophores, tiny pigment organs in the skin that open and close (PubMed Central).

The egi squid jig (angler technique, not research):
• A cloth wrapped body over a glowing or bright core, a weighted nose, and a crown of barbless needle hooks.
• A bright silhouette is easier to see against dark water.

How to work an egi for squid at night, the eging retrieve (angler technique, not research):
• Give three sharp, vertical rod jerks so the squid jig darts upward.
• Pause and let it glide down slowly. Most squid strikes come on the fall.
• Keep the jig working along the shadow line, not in the middle of the light.

Before you go squid fishing, check your local fishing rules, licence requirements and bag limits.
