The world record goliath grouper:
• Lynn Joyner caught a 680 pound goliath grouper on May 20, 1961, off Fernandina Beach, Florida, on a Spanish mackerel bait. It is listed as the IGFA all tackle record (Sport Fishing magazine).
• The Amelia Island Museum of History says he caught it from the Ice Dock on the Amelia River and fought it for about an hour and fifteen minutes.

Where goliath groupers live:
• Adult goliaths hold on nearshore structure such as shipwrecks, ledges and bridge pilings (FWC).
• They eat mostly slow, bottom dwelling prey, above all calico crabs, and generally do not chase fast fish (FWC).
• Researchers describe the link between goliaths and shipwrecks as well established (Gulf and Caribbean Fisheries Institute, 2010).

The goliath grouper rig (angler technique, not research):
• Heavy cable leader: one goliath rig uses 480 pound, 49 strand stainless cable.
• Big circle hook: 16/0 on that rig; one charter uses 10/0 to 12/0 circle hooks on 200 pound leaders.
• A whole fish for bait.
• In Florida, natural bait for goliath must be fished on non offset, non stainless circle hooks, and you must carry a dehooking device (FWC).

The strike and the fight (angler technique, not research):
• Goliaths feed by suction, flaring the mouth to draw in water and prey, as most fish do (Wainwright and colleagues, 2007).
• Lock the drag down hard and turn the fish's head before it gets back into the wreck, or it will break you off.

Release practice: big goliaths must stay in the water and be released right away; Florida's limited harvest is a lottery with a small size slot. Check the current FWC rules before you fish.
