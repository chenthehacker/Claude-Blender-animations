# Sources for the record goliath grouper masterclass

Every on-screen fact and every claim in the voiceover and caption, with where it comes from. Lines marked "angler
technique" are common angling advice, not research. The goliath (modelled about 2.8 m with the tail, illustrative and
not the record fish to scale), the shipwreck, the cross section (not to scale), the rig, the boat, the strike and the
turn are simulated and illustrative.

Note on access: the pages below were read through web search results (excerpts) on 4 and 5 November 2026; the notes
paraphrase those excerpts. Lines of the brief's script that the sources did not support were rewritten (see
"Changed from the brief" at the end).

## Beat 1: the record (hook)
- "In 1961, Lynn Joyner hauled a 680 pound goliath grouper out of Florida waters" / "IGFA ALL TACKLE RECORD · MAY 20,
  1961": Sport Fishing magazine lists the 680 lb goliath grouper (then called a jewfish) caught by Lynn Joyner on
  20 May 1961 off Fernandina Beach, Florida, on a Spanish mackerel bait, as the heaviest IGFA record grouper.
  https://www.sportfishingmag.com/10-heaviest-world-record-groupers
  https://a-z-animals.com/blog/discover-the-largest-goliath-grouper-ever-caught-in-florida/
- Where it was caught: the Amelia Island Museum of History's photo record says Joyner caught the fish from the dock
  known as the Ice Dock on the Amelia River and fought it for about an hour and fifteen minutes.
  https://ameliaisland.pastperfectonline.com/Photo/F73F20BA-FF36-4F4F-9F49-498179184342
  The record fish was not caught on a shipwreck: the wreck in the film illustrates the habitat of beat 2.
- "It's still the all tackle world record, more than sixty years later" / "SPORT FISHING MAGAZINE · IGFA RECORDS":
  the sources above still list it as the all tackle record; the newest one found is from 2023, so its status in
  November 2026 was not confirmed against the IGFA records search. 2026 minus 1961 = 65 years ("more than sixty").
- Pin "goliath grouper": label of the simulated fish.

## Beat 2: the structure, badge [1] WRECK CAVITY STRUCTURE
- "They don't chase bait across open water" / "FWC SPECIES PROFILE": the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation
  Commission describes goliaths as opportunistic predators of slow, bottom dwelling prey, mostly calico crabs; they
  occasionally take fish struggling on a line but generally do not chase fast, free swimming fish.
  https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/profiles/saltwater/grouper/goliath-grouper/
- "They hold tight to shipwrecks and bridge pilings, using dark cavities as ambush cover" / "FWC · GULF AND
  CARIBBEAN FISHERIES INSTITUTE": FWC lists nearshore structure such as wrecks, ledges and bridge pilings as adult
  habitat; a Gulf and Caribbean Fisheries Institute proceedings abstract (2010) says the association of goliaths with
  artificial reefs, especially shipwrecks, is well established. https://proceedings.gcfi.org/?p=83566
  "Ambush cover" is the script's wording for their habit of holding in cavities, not a measured claim.
- Pin "crabs: their main prey": FWC profile above (calico crabs). The drawn crabs are generic.
- Pins "dark cavity", "steel hull": labels of the drawn cross section. "DIAGRAM NOT TO SCALE" applies.

## Beat 3: the rig, badge [2] THE HEAVY RIG
- "Standard gear won't hold" (angler technique): a grouper tackle guide says you need the backbone to stop a fish
  before it reaches structure, and that 60 to 80 lb leaders are typical for ordinary grouper; goliath rigs run much
  heavier (below). https://thetackleroom.com/blogs/news/grouper-fishing-guide-how-to-catch-gag-red-black-grouper
- "Heavy cable leaders, big circle hooks" / pins "heavy cable leader", "big circle hook", "crimped sleeve" (angler
  technique): a goliath hand line rig sold by Dogfish Tackle is crimped to 480 lb 49 strand stainless cable and a
  16/0 Mustad in line circle hook; a Cape Coral charter describes 200 lb leaders with 10/0 to 12/0 circle hooks;
  Mustad sells a giant in line circle hook made for catch and release of giant goliath grouper.
  https://dogfishtacklecompany.com/products/goliath-grouper-hand-line-rig
  https://guidesly.com/book-a-guide/guide-details/Southern-Charter-Co/goliath-grouper-fishing-in-cape-coral-waters
  https://mustad-fishing.com/us/product/39937nnp
  Florida's rules (FWC, as summarised in search results) require natural bait to be fished on non offset, non
  stainless steel circle hooks when fishing for goliath, and a dehooking device to be carried and used.
  https://myfwc.com/fishing/saltwater/recreational/goliath/
- "and a whole fish for bait" / pin "whole fish bait" (angler technique): whole fish baits are standard for
  goliaths; Joyner's record fish took a Spanish mackerel (Sport Fishing, above). The drawn bait is a generic jack
  shaped fish, not a specific species.

## Beat 4: the strike and the fight, badge [3] LOCK THE DRAG
- "They feed by suction, flaring that huge mouth to inhale prey" / "SUCTION FEEDING: WAINWRIGHT ET AL. 2007":
  suction feeding, rapidly expanding the mouth cavity to draw in water and prey, is the dominant mode of prey capture
  in ray finned fishes. Wainwright P, Carroll AM, Collar DC, Day SW, Higham TE, Holzman RA (2007) Suction feeding
  mechanics, performance, and diversity in fishes. Integrative and Comparative Biology 47(1): 96 to 106.
  https://biomechanics.ucr.edu/Wainwright_2007_ICB.pdf
  The paper is about fishes in general; no goliath specific measurement is claimed.
- "Lock your drag down hard and turn the fish's head before it reaches the wreck, or it will break you off" / pins
  "drag locked", "rod doubled over", "head turned" (angler technique): search results report a Florida captain
  fishing goliaths with drag set at 40 to 50 lb who warned that an under tackled goliath will break you off; grouper
  guides advise heavy, smooth drag to keep fish off the rocks and to stop the first run fast. The exact page of the
  40 to 50 lb figure was not confirmed, so the film gives no number.
  https://www.boatus.com/expert-advice/expert-advice-archive/2020/april/fishing-for-goliath-groupers
  https://thetackleroom.com/blogs/news/grouper-fishing-bottom-rigs-what-gear-do-you-actually-need
- "suction strike": label of the simulated strike.

## Outro
- "Stop them early, keep them in the water" / "FWC: RELEASE GOLIATHS IN THE WATER": FWC rules say goliaths over the
  harvest slot must not be removed from the water and must be released immediately (photos must not delay release).
  Florida closed goliath harvest in 1990 and opened a limited lottery harvest in 2023 (outside southeast Florida,
  March to May, a 24 to 36 inch slot); larger fish may not be harvested.
  https://myfwc.com/fishing/saltwater/recreational/goliath/
  https://myfwc.com/news/all-news/goliath-comm-322/
  Rules change: check FWC before fishing.

## What is simulated
The whole film is computer generated: the goliath, its gape and gill flare, the shipwreck and its cut away cross
section, the crabs and baitfish, the rig on its turntable, the boat, rod and reel, the strike, the turn, the silt,
the sea and the light. It is not footage of Joyner's fish or of any real catch.

## Changed from the brief
- The brief's "turn their head in the first three seconds" and "jack crevalle" bait were not supported by any source
  found; the script says "turn the fish's head before it reaches the wreck" and "a whole fish for bait".
- The record was caught from a dock on the Amelia River, not on a wreck; the film does not say it was a wreck catch.
- The film runs about 44 s (paced to the voiceover) instead of 45 s.

## Caption extras
- "the heavyweight of wreck fishing": caption wording, not a ranking.
- "as most fish do": Wainwright et al. (2007) describe suction feeding as the dominant prey capture mode in ray
  finned fishes.
