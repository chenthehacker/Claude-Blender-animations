Stop carrying a 50 pound tackle box full of junk you never use. Here is how to pack a lighter box that still covers every situation.

The core layout (angler technique, not research):
• Organize by water column, not by brand: surface baits in the top tray, mid column baits in the middle, bottom baits in the bin.
• Top tray, topwaters: retail guides call early morning, late evening and overcast days the best topwater windows, though bass anglers report surface bites all day.
• Middle tray, suspending jerkbaits: pause them in the strike zone, and work them faster through water that looks less productive.
• Bottom bin, jigs: let a jig fall to the bottom and bounce or drag it along rocks and hard structure.

The clarity rule:
• Murky water: the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources says size, shape, action and contrast matter most when the water is colored or dirty. Fish also sense vibration through their lateral line. Guides disagree on bright versus dark colors; both give contrast.
• Clear water (angler technique): natural, subtle and translucent ghost patterns are the usual choice.

The forage match (angler technique):
• Match your lure's size and profile to the local baitfish, and downsize when the forage is small. Treat it as a starting rule; bass also hit baits that look nothing like the forage.

Fish light. Pack by the water column. Let clarity and the local bait choose the lure.
