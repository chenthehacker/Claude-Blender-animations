# Sources for the on-screen text and the voiceover

Every factual line in the video (overlays and voiceover) is listed here with where it comes from, and how
strong that source is. Most of this video is angler technique: advice from fishing guides and tackle
retailers, not research findings. Lines marked "technique" are that kind of advice, and the caption says so.

How this was checked: the build machine's network policy blocks most of these sites (state agency pages
too), so the wording below was confirmed from the text of those pages as quoted in web search results on
17 October 2026. Where sources disagree, that is stated.

## Voiceover and on-screen script

The voiceover text is the brief's script, read by a generated text to speech voice (see "What is
simulated"). Two edits were made for the voice only: "50-pound" is spelled "fifty pound" so it is spoken,
and the dash after "brand" became a full stop.

## Beat 1: the hook

**"Stop carrying a 50-pound tackle box full of junk."** The brief's hook line, used as written. It is a
figure of speech, not a measured weight: no source was looked for or found for any tackle box weighing
50 pounds, and the video does not claim one does.

## Beat 2: the core layout (technique)

**"Organize by water column, not brand."** The brief's own organising rule (technique, an opinion). No
source was found that tests organising by water column against organising by brand. It is presented as
a packing method, not a fact.

**"Top tray: surface topwaters" / "for low light feeding."** Dick's Sporting Goods, Topwater Bass Baits
guide: "the best time to use topwater bass baits is during early morning or late evening when bass are
actively feeding near the surface. Additionally, overcast days can also be ideal."
https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/a/topwater-bass-baits-1aaz00a.html
Bass forum anglers say the same, and also report topwater bites all day (Bass Resource forums), so this is
a tendency, not a rule. Technique, retail source.

**"Middle: suspending jerkbaits" / "to cover ground."** Game & Fish, on fishing suspending jerkbaits, quoting
pro angler Mike McClelland: "the goal is to stall a bait in the strike zone to trigger a bite, but to keep
covering ground by working it faster through less productive patches of water."
https://www.gameandfishmag.com/editorial/suspend-a-jerkbait-spring-bass-fishing/375748
Note: the same articles stress long pauses, especially in cold water, so a suspending jerkbait is not
mainly a fast search bait. "To cover ground" is the brief's wording and holds only in the sense quoted.

**"Bottom: heavy jigs."** Minnesota Department of Natural Resources, How to catch smallmouth bass: a jig
"allowed to fall to the bottom then bounce it along the rocky structure"; jigs work best in deeper water.
https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/gofishing/how-catch-smallmouth-bass.html
Field & Stream, Jig fishing for bass: football jigs "are designed to be dragged slowly along a fairly
clean bottom in deeper water". https://www.fieldandstream.com/fishing/jig-fishing-for-bass/
"Anchor your setup" (voiceover) is the brief's figure of speech.

## Beat 3: the clarity rule

**"Murky water? High contrast, heavy vibration."** Minnesota DNR, MinnAqua lesson "Lure Colors": color
choice matters less "if the water is colored or dirty. The most important factors under these conditions
are lure size, shape, action – and contrast."
https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/minnaqua/et/lure-colors.html
Vibration: fish sense water movement through the lateral line, which lets them locate prey in murky water
where visibility is limited (MeatEater, Understand Lateral Lines; Outdoor Canada, on the lateral line
"sixth sense"). https://themeateater.com/fish/gear-fish/understand-lateral-lines-to-help-you-catch-more-fish
https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/sixthsense/
Disagreement: the voiceover says "brights". Guides split on this: some recommend bright and fluorescent
colors (chartreuse, orange) for stained water, others dark colors (black, black and blue). Both are high
contrast. The on-screen line says "high contrast" for that reason. No study was found on rattles.

**"Crystal clear? Natural ghost patterns."** Tackle retailer and angler guides agree on natural, subtle
and translucent ("ghost") colors for clear water (Academy Sports, Lure color: muddy vs clear water;
Bass Resource, Lure colors and water clarity). Technique.
https://www.academy.com/expert-advice/lure-color-muddy-vs-clear-water
https://bassresource.com/fishing/lure-colors-clarity.html
"Line-shy fish" (voiceover only) is the brief's wording. No source was found for it, and it is not shown on
screen.

Badge [2] subtext "let water clarity pick the color" summarises the lines above.

## Beat 4: the forage match (technique)

**"Match your profile to the local forage."** Bassmaster, Lure styling and profiling: "If you're trying
to match the hatch, the overall shape and profile of a lure should also be of prime concern," and its
author matches "the size and profile of the forage" before color. https://bassmaster.com/news/lure-styling-and-profiling

**"Small baitfish? Downsize."** Wired2Fish, Matching forage size for schooling bass (Dustin Connell: a
downsized 2.5 to 3 inch soft plastic for small forage, larger when the baitfish grow), and Outdoor News:
scaling lures down to match the forage gets more bites under tough conditions.
https://www.wired2fish.com/fishing-tips/matching-forage-size-for-schooling-bass
Exceptions exist (big bright baits in dirty spring water), so this is a starting rule, not a law.
"Immediately" (voiceover) is the brief's emphasis.

The animated swimbait is scaled to the length of the animated baitfish (62 percent of its starting size);
these are illustrative sizes, not recommended ones.

## What is simulated

Everything on screen is computer generated: the tackle box, every lure, hook and tool, the baitfish, the
studio and the water backgrounds. The lures are generic types (popper, walking bait, jerkbait, paddle
tail swimbait, jig, craw, stick worm, spinnerbait), not copies of any brand's product. The murky and clear
water backgrounds, the vibration rings and the sparkles are illustrations. The voiceover is a synthetic
voice generated with Higgsfield text to speech, not a recorded person.
